Essence

For the wildly successful, but quietly disconnected.

Leah Roling is a personal brand for accomplished adults who have built the life they thought they wanted — and discovered, somewhere along the way, that they stopped being present in it. The work is the long, undramatic return: from doing back to being, from the curated life back to the lived one.

Positioning

Most personal-development brands sell more. Leah's sells less, but real.

The brand is not aspirational performance. It is the soft, persistent invitation to come home to your own life — friendlier than therapy, more grounded than self-help, and unafraid of the truths most successful people are too busy to look at.

Voice

Editorial, unhurried, intimate. Speaks in plain sentences but lets a few of them land like poetry. Coaches without lecturing. Challenges without confronting. Allows for grief and humor in the same paragraph. Never sells from urgency. Never moralizes. Trusts the reader to do the work.

Sounds like
  • "You have forgotten you have dreams of your own."
  • "There are no balls. No one is juggling."
  • "How you lead is another question."
  • "Wrong people want to be inspired."
Never sounds like
  • "Crush your goals."
  • "Manifest your best life."
  • "Hustle harder."
  • "You've got this, queen."
Palette

A five-color system. The amber leads. The peach softens. The blush lifts on dark backgrounds. Together they read as warm, sunlit, late-afternoon-honest — never bright, never girlish, never corporate.

Anchor Lines
  • For the wildly successful, but quietly disconnected.
  • If you woke up with breath this morning — you lead.
  • You'd stop scrolling the lives of others — and start experiencing your own.
  • There are no balls. No one is juggling.
  • The dream did not disappear. It has been within, all along.
  • Wrong people want to be inspired. Right people want to be changed.
Applications

Website & long-form essays: warm ivory base, amber italic emphasis, generous whitespace, Fraunces display with Instrument Sans body. Letterboxed cold-opens are part of the brand.

Social: single-color cards (ivory or charcoal background), one italic line, no logo lockup. The phrase is the brand.

Books & print: deckle-edge feel, single-column type, amber accents reserved for chapter italics. Page numbers in Fraunces italic.

Avoid: stock photography of women looking inspirationally at sunsets. Bright/poppy colors. Hustle iconography. Gratuitous emoji.

Essence

The architecture of self-leadership.

InnerBoard is the umbrella for four products that share one operating thesis: most adults were taught to manage their lives, never to lead them. The Board is the internal cabinet of selves — Protector, Critic, Dreamer, CEO — that runs every life whether or not the person knows it.

Positioning

School taught you algebra. Nobody taught you how to lead yourself.

InnerBoard occupies the space between coaching, education, and culture. It is not therapy. It is not a course platform. It is the missing curriculum for the human underneath every job, role, relationship, and decision.

Voice

Plain-spoken systems-thinking. Methodology over motivation. Identity over tactics. The voice is the calm certainty of someone who has done the work and refuses to dress it up. Confident without grandiosity.

Sounds like
  • "Become the person who creates it."
  • "The thought drives the result."
  • "Change the person who was behaving."
  • "Standards over motivation."
Never sounds like
  • "Unlock your potential."
  • "Transform in 30 days."
  • "Find your why."
  • "Live your truth."
Palette

A single rose accent paired with a deep warm charcoal off-palette. The umbrella reads warmer than its product children. Rose is the family color — every product page borrows from it without copying it.

  • Already know what you want. Now learn to be the person who creates it.
  • Thoughts drive emotions. Emotions drive actions. Actions create results.
  • School taught you algebra. Nobody taught you how to lead yourself.
  • Change your behavior — or change the person who was behaving.
Applications

Umbrella website: the homepage. Houses the family. Sends visitors downstream to the right product. Rose accent.

Cross-channel: InnerBoard branding appears in product footers as "part of the InnerBoard ecosystem." The umbrella never competes with its children — it identifies them.

Essence

The manual school never gave you.

Academy is the self-paced library of every framework, philosophy, and inner mechanic that powers the InnerBoard methodology. Where InnerLab is practice, Academy is curriculum. Cerebral. Catalog-shaped. Built for the reader, the student, the quiet learner.

Positioning

Most courses teach you something new. Academy teaches you to see something true.

Frameworks aren't ideas to memorize. They are mirrors. Academy is the place where someone walks in for information and walks out with self-recognition.

Voice

Academic without being dry. Library-quiet. Speaks the way a great teacher does — with the patience to let the reader arrive at the answer themselves. Never hype. Never tactic-of-the-week.

Sounds like

  • "Master this in 7 days."

  • "Quick wins."
  • "Limited-time offer."
Palette

Dark forest teal as primary, paired with burnished bronze and wheat cream off-palette. Together they describe an entire library: teal walls, bronze lamps, cream pages. Library atmosphere, not just library accents.

Pairings · When two brands appear together
Leah + InnerBoard
The personal brand and the umbrella. Amber + rose are first cousins in the warm-earth family — they harmonize without competing. The default pairing whenever Leah introduces the methodology.
InnerBoard + Academy
Rose and dark teal are nature's classic complement — botanical, library-aesthetic, sophisticated. Use when introducing Academy as a product of InnerBoard.
InnerBoard + InnerLab
Same family of pinks, different temperatures. Rose is the contemplative parent, vibrant pink is the alive child. Works beautifully at different scales — never side-by-side at equal weight.
Academy + INQ
The library and the credential. Dark teal + editorial gold is the institutional pairing — university crest, museum signage. Use whenever Academy and INQ are presented as the academic pair.
OS + INQ
Steel blue and gold — corporate boardroom meets credential. Use for institutional-grade collateral, executive coaching materials, and any moment where authority and B2B credibility share the page.
Leah + InnerLab
The personal brand and the membership. Peach-amber softens into the vibrant pink — a warm sunrise into full daylight. The pairing for InnerLab member welcome materials and onboarding emails written in Leah's voice.

Pairings to avoid.

InnerLab pink + INQ gold reads as wedding-invitation, not brand-system. Two saturated jewel tones competing. If both must appear, demote one to a small accent.
Vibrant pink + steel blue reads as tech-conference badge. Two cold-warm primaries fighting. Better: pair InnerLab with Academy or with InnerBoard rose, where temperatures rhyme.
Leah amber + INQ gold are too close in temperature — they bleed into each other and lose distinction. If both on the page, separate with significant ivory negative space.
Two cool blues at similar value. Academy and OS rarely share a page — they're meant for different audiences. If they must, give Academy the dominant role and OS a single small accent.

The colors are the easy part. These pages describe what the brands are trying to feel like, what they should never sound like, and how they live together when they share a page.
